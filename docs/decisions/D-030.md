# D-030: A direction is built in the order of its stages (loss, rate, delay with jitter), with two qdiscs where one cannot do it

Accepted. The question was what to do with a scenario that has a jitter and a rate in one direction, which one `netem` qdisc reproduces wrongly (D-029). The answer follows from what the scenario means and from what the kernel can do.

- **What the scenario means is now written down** (`docs/SCENARIO-FORMAT.md`, "Impairments of a direction"): the stages of a direction act as loss, then rate, then delay with jitter, whatever order the keys have. Packets wait for the rate in a queue, in order; each then travels for its own delay; a jitter larger than the gap between packets lets one overtake another. The format said "has this standard deviation" for a normal jitter and said nothing about the order of the stages, so the measured 4.1 ms was a violation of the first sentence and an accident of the second.
- **The four ways out, in the order of the project's rules.** (1) *A notice in the result*: runs the scenario without the author's consent to a result that does not match the numbers, and a notice does not make an approximation any less one. This breaks "a backend never approximates silently" in spirit; rejected. (2) *Refusing the combination*: honest, but it makes every typical link with a jitter and a rate impossible (a mobile link, a satellite hop, a Wi-Fi access: two of the four examples), when the kernel can do it exactly. (3) *Counting it as limited (`~`) and waiting for `allow-limited`*: right for a limit that cannot be removed, and the machinery stays for the day there is one. Not right here, because this limit can be removed. (4) *Making the backend exact*: a prototype (a `netem` with the rate as the root and a `netem` with the delay and jitter as its child) measured 41.11 ms, 5.00 ms and 28.6 % reordered where one qdisc gave 42.71, 4.19 and 0 %; the model of the series gives 41.01, 4.99 and 28.8. **Chosen.** A backend refuses what it cannot do faithfully; this one can.
- **The build** (`netem.rs`): a direction with both a jitter and a rate gets a root qdisc with `limit 1000` (the queue of the link), the loss with its seed and the rate, and a child (`parent 1:1`) with the derived limit for the packets in flight, the delay and the jitter. The loss stays in front of the rate, where one qdisc has always applied it, so the seeded loss patterns do not change. Every other direction keeps its one qdisc, and everything that was measured about them stays as measured.
- **Proof.** The case in the fidelity suite, a canary that judges the numbers of the old behaviour with its bounds (all three fail) and the same canary with the new numbers (they pass), a model with both compositions, the unit tests of the argument builder, and a test of `tc qdisc show` that checks which stage holds what.
- **Not done, and why it matters later.** `apply` builds a direction once, at time zero. The changes over time (release 0.2) must remove the child when a step leaves a direction without a jitter or without a rate, because `tc qdisc replace` of the root keeps the child. A uniform jitter with a rate goes through the same code and is covered by the unit tests, but has no case of its own; the loss together with the two stages is not measured. They are listed in [FIDELITY.md](../FIDELITY.md), "What is not measured yet".

Alternatives: a `tbf` as the rate stage (a token bucket with a burst of at least `rate / HZ`, so it does not serialise packet by packet, which the single-stage `rate` and the model do), a notice and an `allow-limited` for something that can be fixed, and a new `Feature` for the combination (it would turn a thing the backend does exactly into a thing it declares as limited).
