# D-088: The level of a TCP workload is its goodput in a bin, and a bin counts only while the workload is working

Accepted (2026-11-06). For PRB-07 on TCP workloads, which D-086 left open ("the recovery after an event works on bytes").

- **The level of a bin is the goodput in it**, in bits per second: the payload that reached the receiving end in the bin, the server for `tcp-bulk` and the client (the responses, in the bin of the last byte of each) for `request-response`. A UDP stream has a level that is a share (what arrived of what was sent in the bin), because it sends at a pace the scenario sets. A TCP sender sets its own pace by feedback, so there is nothing sent per bin to divide by; the only level that means something is what the workload got through, measured against what it got through before.
- **The rest of the definition of D-070 is kept**, with the same parameters (`measure.recovery`: `baseline-bins`, `window-bins`, `fraction`): the baseline is the median of the levels of the bins before the bin of the disturbance, the level to reach is `fraction` of it, the workload has recovered at the start of the first window of `window-bins` bins, from the bin of the event on, whose mean goodput reaches that level; the time is that start minus the moment of the event, never negative, with the resolution of a bin. The disturbance of an outage's end begins at its start, and that of an `up` at the last `down`. The entry in the result says `basis: "goodput"`, and `baseline` and `level` are in bits per second.
- **Only bins in which the workload was working count.** A bin is working if all of it lies between the start of the first connection and the end of the last one (the completion of the transfer for a `tcp-bulk` with `bytes`, the end of the window for the others). The bins before the workload started, after it finished and the part-filled bins at the edges are no baseline and no window: a transfer that is done has not "dropped to zero". This is the counterpart of D-070's "a bin in which nothing was sent has no level". A transfer that finishes before the level is reached again has no time and says so.
- **The parameter `basis` does not apply.** `send` and `arrival` are two views of packets; a TCP workload has one view of bytes. `--recovery-basis` and `measure.recovery.basis` leave a TCP entry as it is.
- **Which events concern a TCP workload:** the steps on any link of its path, in either direction, because the data go one way and the acknowledgements the other.
- **Slow start is not hidden.** The baseline is the median of the bins before the disturbance, so an event during the ramp-up of a connection has a low baseline and a recovery of 0 s. That is the definition working as written; the entry to read is that of an event after the ramp, and `RESULTS.md` says so, as D-070 does for a `set` that lifts a limit.
- **`request-response` has a goodput too**, the bytes of the responses by bin (D-086 left it out: only the server's progress was there). Its level at a low rate is lumpy in small bins (one response in a bin or none); the bin is the caller's to choose.
- **The metric names** for the sets of runs (`<id>.goodput.mean`, `<id>.completion`, `<id>.latency.p99`, `<id>.failed`, ...) are in `RESULTS.md`; the round trip of the UDP echo (D-087) has the names `<id>.round_trip.*`, the same as the round trip of a request.

Alternatives: the bytes acknowledged to the sender (a sender's view, which includes bytes in flight and the SYN, and says nothing about what the receiver got); the share of the capacity of the link (the run does not know the capacity of a path of several links, queues and rates, and a baseline from the workload's own history is the one D-070 uses); a level of 1 for "the transfer is done" (a finished transfer would then recover at once from every event after it, which says nothing).
