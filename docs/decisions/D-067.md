# D-067: The default wait for the packets in the network is until the queues are empty

Accepted (2026-11-02). It closes the open point of D-027: the default of `settle` was one second and twice the longest hold, and a link with a rate holds the packets that are sent faster than it lets them go for as long as that takes. Measured on the way: 3 mbit of payload into a link of 2 mbit with a delay of 20 ms, for 3 s, 1,125 packets: the default lost **134 (11.9 %)** and a wait of 4 s lost none. They were not lost, they were in the queue when the receivers were told to stop, and the result said they were lost without a word. That is a wrong measurement, and it needs no big queue to happen.

- **The run measures the drain.** `Backend::queue_backlog` (a method with a default of `None`) says how many packets the queues of a direction hold now. Without a wait that is set, the run looks every 50 ms at all directions of all links, and when they have been empty for 300 ms (the grace for the receivers to read what the links let go) it goes on. The kernel's answer is the `qlen` of the root queue of the interface of the sender, and it counts the packets behind the root too: measured with one queue and with two (a rate with a jitter), where the run lost nothing and waited as long as the queue needed. A direction without queues has none.
- **What the field says.** `timing.settle_ns` is the time that the wait took, so it is shorter than the old default for a scenario without a queue (the old default had a second of margin; the new one has 0.3 s after the last packet has left the queue) and longer for one with a queue. A wait that is set is kept as it is. A backend that cannot count gets the old time.
- **The ceiling.** A queue that does not empty in two minutes ends the wait, the packets that are left count as lost, and the result's `notes` say how many were left and that `--settle` can wait longer. Two minutes is the order of the biggest queue the backend builds (1,000 packets of 1,242 bytes at 100 kbit take 100 s); a scenario that needs more is a scenario whose queue is the point.
- **Not a fixed time any more means the time varies from run to run** by the polling and the grace. It never varies the packets: they are the ones that were sent.
- **Tested.** The core with a fake whose queue holds 5, 3, 1, 0, 2 and then 0 packets (the wait does not end at the first empty look), without the count (the old time), with a wait that is set (kept), and at the ceiling (the note). In real namespaces, with one queue and with two: the run with a rate of 2 mbit under 3 mbit loses nothing and waits between 1.6 s and 4.5 s (the queue needs 1.69 s); the canary with the wait set to 100 ms loses most of the 324 packets that were in the queue.

Alternatives: a better formula from the rate and the limit (the limit is a fact of the backend, the backlog depends on the timeline, and a formula is a guess where the queue can be asked); a longer fixed default (every run would wait that long); the receivers deciding (a receiver cannot tell a lost packet from a late one, D-028).
