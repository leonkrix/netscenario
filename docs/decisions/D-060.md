# D-060: The run does the steps of the timeline by the clock and records each one

Accepted (2026-11-02). The first half of D-038, without the barrier: `Scheduled steps` is now declared exact for the Kernel backend (a `set`), and `Outages` and `Link down and up` stay planned. The sender does not wait for a step yet (D-062), so what D-038 promises for the lost packets is **not** promised by this step, and the result says so instead of leaving it to the reader.

- **The loop.** `drive.rs` replaces the wait for the senders. It does the steps of `timeline::steps` when they are due, counted from the moment the workloads start (the `--start-at-ns` of the senders), and polls the senders in between: the first sender that is still going waits for news until the next step is due, the others are only looked at. The wait for news counts whole milliseconds, so within 3 ms of a step the loop sleeps to the exact moment and does the step then (a step is never done before its moment, and on the development machine it began 0.05 ms after it in 10 of 12 steps; the other two were the second direction of a `both`, which waits for the first). The cancel token is looked at before every wait and every step, so a run is cancelled between two steps.
- **The events belong to the scenario up to its end.** The loop goes on until every sender has ended **and** every step is done, also where all workloads stop earlier, so that the result is what the file says. A step that fails ends the run with the error of the backend: a run in which the link did not change would measure something else than the scenario.
- **The record** (`events`): the event, the kind, the link and direction, and three moments from the start of the workloads: scheduled, started (the call to the backend), applied (it returned: the kernel has the change when `tc` has ended). Lateness is `applied - scheduled`; measured on the development machine, a change takes 1.7 to 2.4 ms (a `tc` process started through `nsenter`; S11 measured 1.0 ms on another machine), so a step is 2 to 2.5 ms late and the second direction of a `both` about 4 ms. That is the figure the persistent `tc -batch` is for (0.03 ms). The observer gets a `StepDone` with it. Not recorded yet, though D-038 asks for it: the number of packets that had entered the queue. Reading it costs a second `tc` call and belongs with the barrier, where it proves that the change landed before the intended packet.
- **Reproducibility is said, not assumed.** A step done by the clock lands before a different packet in every run (S11: one packet late idle, up to eight busy). That moves the lost packets only where the packets draw different numbers afterwards, so `timeline::changes_what_is_drawn` is true when a step changes the loss (its value or its model), the duplication, the corruption or the reordering of a direction, and then `seed.loss_reproducible` is `false` with the reason. A step that changes a rate or a delay draws nothing differently and leaves the run reproducible. Not taken: calling every run with a timeline reproducible (the measured fact says it is not), and none (a rate drop is the common case and loses nothing).
- **The bound on the lateness in the tests** was 50 ms at most and 0 at least (D-069 made the upper bound follow the machine, with 50 ms as its floor): noise only goes one way, a step is never early (a test fails if it began before its moment), and the room is more than ten times what S11 measured with every processor busy (a `tc` process through `nsenter`, 3.6 ms). A canary judges made-up records (80 ms late, early, backwards) and must name each. The throughput test is the physical one: 3 mbit offered into 4 mbit, limited to 1 mbit at 3 s, gives 3.00 mbit before and 0.96 after (1 mbit of frames of 1,042 bytes carries 0.9597 mbit of 1,000 byte payloads), judged with two percent.
- **Where the rest goes.** The barrier is D-062, and the persistent `tc -batch` is a step of its own; `outage`, `down`, `up` as steps (D-064); the reason for a second workload in a direction with loss (D-065) and for a direction that no workload sends into (D-062).

Alternatives: a thread for the timeline next to the loop (a second user of the backend, which is single threaded by design); polling the clock with a short sleep (a step up to the poll interval late); applying the steps before the senders start (not a timeline).
