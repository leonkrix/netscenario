# D-101: Netlink is a thin layer of our own over `netlink-packet-route` and `netlink-sys`, with the options of the qdiscs encoded by us and checked against `tc`

Accepted (2026-11-12), for BKD-05. S22 built the messages for `netem` (delay, jitter, limit, loss with correlation, Gilbert-Elliott, duplication, corruption, reordering with a gap, rate, `ecn`, `seed`), `pfifo`, `bfifo`, `codel`, `pie`, `fq_codel`, `tbf` and `htb` with a class and a leaf, applied them in a user namespace, and compared the dump of the kernel (`tc -d qdisc show`, `class show`) with what the `tc` command makes of the same settings: **17 of 17 are identical** (handles that the kernel hands out and drawn seeds left out). A change of a running `netem` took **2.8 microseconds** (20,000 changes; D-014 measured 12 for `nlink` and `tc -batch`).

- **The libraries.** `netlink-packet-route` 0.34.0 (2026-11-03): 52,000 lines, no `unsafe`, synchronous, dependencies `bitflags`, `libc`, `log`, `netlink-packet-core`, `zerocopy`; it builds links, addresses, routes, `fq_codel`, `ingress`, `mirred` and the `u32`, `flower` and `matchall` filters, and it has **no `netem`, `htb`, `tbf`, `codel`, `pie` or fifos** (checked on 2026-11-12). The options of those are encoded by us from `linux/pkt_sched.h` (about 400 lines) and sent as raw attributes (`TcAttribute::Other`; `netem` is the one qdisc whose options begin with a plain struct, not an attribute). `netlink-sys` 0.9.0 is the socket. The 64-bit attributes for the latency and the jitter of `netem` carry nanoseconds, so no conversion to scheduler ticks is needed there; `tbf` and `htb` need the ticks of 64 nanoseconds, which the layer computes.
- **Not `nlink`.** `nlink` 0.33.0 (2026-10-23, one maintainer, 26,000 downloads) has 227,000 lines, 106 places with `unsafe`, requires `tokio` and `tracing`, and changes its API between minor versions; the backend is synchronous and would take an async runtime for it.
- **Namespaces.** A socket belongs to the namespace in which it was made, so the layer makes the socket of a node in a thread that has entered the namespace (`setns`, in `nix`, safe) and leaves it again; the `sysctl`s are written to `/proc/sys` the same way. This was not part of S22 and is the first thing the step builds and tests.
- **What stays outside.** `unshare`, `nsenter` and `prlimit` of util-linux are still used by the supervisor; replacing them is not part of this release. `ethtool` is not needed (D-098).
- **The jitter tables** (`normal`, `pareto`, `paretonormal`) are read from the files of the system as now (D-084) and sent in the attribute of the distribution, or made by us from their definition; the step chooses by comparing the tables byte for byte.
- **What it removes.** The requirement for iproute2 6.6.0 or newer (the `seed` is an attribute the kernel reads, not a feature of `tc`), the CI step that builds iproute2 6.15 for every job (the step that stopped the CI for a day on 2026-11-11 when the mirror of the packages could not be reached), and the text of ENV-07 about `tc` knowing `seed`.
- **The proof.** Every test of the Kernel backend passes unchanged; a golden test per message against what `tc` sends; the state read back equals the state of the `tc` path for each scenario of `scenarios/`.

Alternatives: `nlink` pinned exactly (a very large asynchronous dependency with `unsafe` inside); `rtnetlink` (has no `netem`); keeping `ip` and `tc` (a tool that must be installed, a new enough version for the seed, a process for every change).
