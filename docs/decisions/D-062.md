# D-062: A step of the timeline is done at a barrier of the workload, so the same seed gives the same lost packets

Accepted (2026-11-02). It carries out D-038. The persistent `tc -batch` of the same decision is left out of this step on purpose: it is performance (a change takes 2 ms as a process and 0.03 ms in a batch), it has a failure mode of its own (the hang that S11 saw on a runner), and the guarantee does not depend on it.

- **The mechanism.** The run gives every step of a direction that a workload sends into to the first such workload, and works out, with the integer arithmetic of the sender's own clock (`Stream::packets_in`), the number of the first packet at or after the moment of the step. The sender is started with `--barriers packet:id,...` (the id is the place of the step in `timeline::steps`). Right after it has sent the packet before, it prints `barrier`; the loop of the run (`drive.rs`) does the step through `Backend::change` and answers `go <id>`; at the slot of the packet the sender goes on, waiting only if the answer is not there yet. A step is done at most one gap before its moment, never between two packets that the moment lies between.
- **Measured.** A rate of 3 mbit in packets of 1,000 bytes (a gap of 2.67 ms), a step at 1 s: the sender asked after packet 374 at 997.4 ms, the change took 2.0 ms and was done at 999.4 ms, so packet 375 at 1,000 ms left on time (it waited 1.0 to 1.7 microseconds). With the backend made to take 40 ms and 120 ms for the change, the packet waited 40 and 120 ms and the same packets were lost, 3 runs out of 3: the lost packets of a run in which the loss comes on at 1 s (no draw before, 30 % after, the sharpest case because every draw after the step moves with its place) are the same sequence numbers, none before packet 375. The canary without the barrier (`RunOptions::barriers = false`) with 80 ms for the change gives other lost packets. Both pass under 24 busy loops on 4 cores.
- **The loop serves barriers first.** A step by the clock no longer sleeps to its moment while a sender may still ask for a barrier: the sleep (up to 3 ms) held a packet back and let the step by the clock overtake the barrier before it (found when a `both` listed its directions in the wrong order). The loop waits for news a millisecond at a time instead, and does the step by the clock when its moment has come.
- **What is left to the clock, and what the result says.** A step no sender can wait for: no workload sends into the direction, or the moment lies after the last packet (then nothing of the workload meets it, and the clock does as well). A timeline that changes what a queue draws random numbers for (the loss, the duplication, the corruption, the reordering) makes the run not reproducible, with the reason, in three cases: the step is done by the clock because **no workload sends into the direction**, the barriers are off. (Two workloads in one direction are a reason of their own for the whole run since D-065, and no longer one of the steps.) A change of a rate or a delay draws nothing differently and says nothing against the run.
- **A sender that goes on without an answer fails the run.** If the run is too slow to answer in 10 seconds the sender goes on and counts it (`barrier_timeouts`); the run turns that into an error, because a result that says "the same seed gives the same lost packets" would be false for that step. A sender that has ended without asking a barrier is an error too.
- **`RunOptions::barriers`** (default `true`) exists for the canary and for a person who wants to see what the clock does; `false` is not a mode to run tests in.
- **The record.** `events` has `packet` (the number of packets that met the old state) and `held_ns` for a step at a barrier. The number of packets in the queue that D-038 asked to record is the same number for the workload that is bound, and is known without a second `tc` call.

Alternatives: the clock with a note (what 0.2 did first, the simplest, and not what the project promises); a thread for the barriers next to the loop (the backend is single threaded by design, and a lock around `change` would add a wait to every poll); asking every barrier at the start of the run (the sender would have to know the whole timeline, and a change of the file mid-run is not meant).
