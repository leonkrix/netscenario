# D-064: An outage is a window at the delivery, a link that is down refuses, and the statistics know which packets were refused

Accepted (2026-11-02). It carries out D-039 and D-050 for the Kernel backend: `Outages` and `Link down and up` are exact now. An outage is two steps, its start and its end, that touch no value of the direction (`Action::Blackout` in `netscenario_core::timeline`, `Backend::plan_outage` before the run and `Backend::set_outage` for each step; the `outage` flag of `Action::Impair` stays and says whether an outage is on at that moment, which its impairments no longer contain); a link that goes down or comes up is a call of its own, `Backend::set_link` (a method with a default that says no), and in the Kernel backend `ip link set <interface> down` or `up` at both ends of the link.

- **An outage is a window in the time of the receiver.** For the length of the outage nothing is delivered, **also not a packet that was on its way when it began**; the sender notices nothing. It is what the ledger promised ("windows without any delivery") and what a person who writes `outage: 2s` expects: with a delay of 600 ms (a satellite) an outage that lets the packets in flight arrive would show traffic for the first 600 ms of it. The first version of this step did the other thing, a loss of 100 % when a packet enters the queue, and changed the title of LNK-12 to fit. That is a legitimate thing too (a radio that is quiet loses what it is given), and it is exact and reproducible, but it is what `set: { forward: { loss: 100% } }` and `loss: null` do already; an `outage` that is only that is sugar, and the gap, "silently drop everything, also what is on its way", stays open. `down` drops what is in flight too, but the sender gets errors.
- **How it is built.** A direction with an outage in its timeline gets one more `netem`, the **blackout**, as the child of its last queue (`netem.rs`): the only queue, or the second one when the direction has a rate and a jitter or a reordering (D-030). A `netem` hands a packet to its child when the delay of the packet is over, so a loss of 100 % in the blackout drops what is on its way at that moment and what leaves in the window, and nothing in front of it knows: the loss, the duplication, the corruption and the reordering draw their numbers as they did, with their seeds. It has no delay, draws no number that a seed is to repeat (a loss of 100 % or of 0 % has one outcome) and is written whole each time (`limit 1000 loss random 0.000000%` or `100.000000%`). It is in the tree for the whole run, also while no outage is going on, because building it later would drop the packets in flight and start the random numbers over (D-059). A change of the impairments never writes it (`Stages::change` has none), so a `set` does not end an outage, and ending an outage touches nothing else (tested: every state is written while the outage is on and off, and the queues are those of a fresh direction).
- **Measured.** A spike first (`tc` 6.15, 100 ms delay, a packet every 10 ms, the blackout switched to 100 % at 1.0 s and back at 1.5 s): the lost packets were 91 to 140, the longest quiet time at the receiver 0.510 s and it began at 1.000 s, so the packets sent in the 100 ms before the window were dropped. Then the run: 375 packets a second, a delay of 100 ms, an outage from 2 s to 4 s: the lost packets were 713 to 1,462 in three runs out of three, 750 of 2,250, one run without a gap, the 37 packets that were in flight at 2 s among them (an outage at the entrance loses 750 to 1,499 and nothing before), and the bounds that the recorded moments of the two steps allow were 710 to 716 and 1,459 to 1,466.
- **The count of the queue holds.** The packets that the blackout drops are in the counters of the first queue, as the packets that its own loss drops are: `packets + drops + qlen` of the root is the number of packets that entered, in the layout of one queue and in that of two (measured: 306 for 300 sent and 6 of IPv6 that the spike did not switch off; the check of the run agrees in both). I had expected to have to add the drops of the blackout, and the measurement said no.
- **The edges are placed by the clock, and the result says so.** The window is in the time of the receiver, and no packet of the sender can wait for it, so the two steps are done by the clock (they are not bound to a barrier) and the packet at each edge can be lost in one run and delivered in another: the step is never early, and it is a few tenths of a millisecond late, against a gap of 2.7 ms. Measured: the same packets in three runs, but it is not promised. The result has `seed.loss_reproducible` `false` with a reason for a scenario with an outage, as it has for corruption (D-055), where a few packets differ in the same way. The packets that the seed loses are the same, and the test of the same seed compares them outside the edges. An outage is not a step that changes what a queue draws numbers for (`changes_what_is_drawn`), because the queues that draw do not know of it.
- **The public API only grew.** The check of the pull request compares with `main`, which has the first version of the timeline; the variant `Blackout` is the last of `Action` (the others keep their numbers), `Action::Impair` keeps its `outage` field, `plan_states` keeps its parameters and the outage has a method of its own, and `Probability::ALWAYS` stays. The first version of this change broke those four, and each was avoidable at no cost.
- **The rule of D-050 that a `loss` must not be set inside an outage is gone**, with its example `timeline-loss-in-outage.yaml` and the snapshot of its message: the reason was that an outage was a loss of 100 % and "which value wins" had no answer. An outage is not a value now. The rule that outages of one direction do not overlap stays: the end of the first would end the second.
- **A duplicating `netem` next to the blackout.** Some kernels refuse a duplicating `netem` next to another one in a tree (D-052). A direction with a duplication and an outage is such a tree, so the kernel is asked as for a rate with a jitter (the same probe, the same refusal with the answer of the kernel, naming the duplication), and the refusal says that the outage is among the reasons.
- **A link that is down refuses.** The interface of both ends is down, not without carrier (D-039 chose "the sender gets errors"): a send on either side fails with "Network is unreachable", the agent counts it, and the addresses and the queues stay. The fixed neighbours and the routes that lead out of the interface do not: the kernel removes them with the interface (found with the networks of several nodes, D-078), so the backend puts them back when the link comes up; without the neighbours the first packet of each side would wait for an ARP exchange across the impaired link, and without the routes nothing would be carried again. The kernel drops the packets that are in the queues when the interface goes down, silently: no counter of the queue has them (measured in a spike, delay 100 ms, a packet every 10 ms, the interface down for 0.2 s: 280 packets were handed to the kernel, 20 were refused, and the queue counted 272, so the 8 that were in flight are in no counter). Each step costs two `ip` processes (about 4 ms, shown by `events`); a link that goes down is not a hot path.
- **The statistics were wrong for refused packets, and are not any more.** The sender uses the sequence number of a packet that the kernel refuses all the same (a packet has its place in time), but the statistics assumed that the numbers are `0` up to `sent` minus one. With a link down for a second they counted 375 packets as unexpected and 379 as lost. Found by the first test of `down`. Now the sender lists the refused packets as runs (`refused`, at most 10,000, more fails the run, because it could not say which packets were sent), `analyse_refused` and `loss_runs_refused` and `name_damaged_refused` leave them out (not sent, not lost, not a break in a run of lost packets, and an arrival with their number is `unexpected`), the packet export has `refused` lines, and the result has `sender.refused`. The old functions are the new ones with no refused packets, so nothing breaks.
- **Barriers for `down` and `up`.** They are bound to the first workload that sends over the link in either direction, at the packet that follows their moment, so the number of refused packets is exact (375 in the test). A link with a workload in each direction is bound to the first; the other direction meets the step wherever the machine puts it.
- **Reproducibility of `down` and `up`.** They change what a queue draws numbers for: the packets that the interface refuses never reach the queue and draw none. The packets in flight when the interface goes down are another matter: which they are depends on the timing of the queue, so the lost packets of a seed can differ by those at the border, and the count of the queue (D-036) cannot hold, because the flushed packets are in no counter. For a link that goes down and has a direction that draws random numbers, the check of the queue is not made and the result says why (`not_reproducible_because`).
- **Tested.** Outage: the lost packets are one run that begins before the moment of the outage by the packets in flight and ends within the bounds of the recorded steps; nothing arrives in the window; the quiet time; the steps are by the clock; the reason; a `set` inside the window is done and its values go on after it; two queues with a loss and the count of the queue; the same seed loses the same packets outside the edges. Down and up: 375 refused packets as the run `[750, 1124]`, none lost but the few in flight, nothing arrives that was refused, everything arrives again, the reason in the result. The blackout queue: its place in the tree in both layouts, that a change does not write it, that it is independent of the states, that a direction that was not planned for an outage refuses one. Statistics: refused packets are not lost, a canary that reads the same arrivals without them gives other numbers, an arrival of a refused number, the loss runs across a refusal, the damaged packets, the export lines, the sender's runs and their limit.

Alternatives: a loss of 100 % at the entrance, as the first version had it (see above: it is `set loss`, and it leaves what is in flight); a flush of the queue at the start (`qdisc replace` drops the packets in flight, but it loses the state of the queue and its random numbers, D-048, D-059, and the packets would not be counted anywhere); a drop at the interface of the receiver with a filter (not tried: as far as I know the packet socket of the tap and the counters of the interface see a frame before a filter drops it, and then the accounting of D-040 would call the packets that the outage drops damaged ones); the window at the entrance shifted by the delay (exact only for a fixed delay without a rate, a jitter or a queue, and the steps would have to be made before the moment of the file); `down` as the loss of the carrier only (a sender sees no error, which is what a pulled cable shows, but D-039 and the tests of an application's handling of errors want the error); the refused packets taking no sequence number (the statistics need nothing then, but a packet would lose its place in time and the numbers of `events` and of the result would not mean the same packet); a queue count that subtracts the flush (the number is not known).
