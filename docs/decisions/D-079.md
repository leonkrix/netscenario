# D-079: A link that needs a queue discipline or a rate that changes is built through a fabric namespace, with a delaying `netem` in front of the shaper

Accepted (2026-11-03). Release 0.3 adds queue limits and disciplines (LNK-09), kernel statistics of the queues (PRB-10) and rates that follow a trace (LMD-03). The Kernel backend built every direction from `netem` (D-030); the experiments of the shaper, the queue and the fabric (runs on Ubuntu 22.04 with kernel 6.8 and 24.04 with kernel 7.0, `tc` 7.3.0) show why that cannot go on for these three, and what replaces it. The maintainer chose the exact way over the simple one.

- **`netem` cannot follow a rate that changes.** It fixes the moment a packet leaves when the packet enters (D-070). On a staircase between 6 and 2 mbit it delivered 75 % of the bytes at steps of 100 ms down to 1 ms; after a drop to 1.2 mbit from 3 s to 5 s the low rate stayed in force for two more seconds and the largest delay was 3,569 ms against 653 ms for a shaper. CoDel, FQ-CoDel and PIE need a queue of their own, which `netem` does not have.
- **The shaper is an `htb` class** with equal `rate` and `ceil` and a small burst, with the discipline (`pfifo`, `codel`, `fq_codel`, `pie`, ...) as its leaf. A `tbf` refills its bucket at every change (27 % too many bytes at steps of 20 ms), an `htb` class keeps its tokens: exact at 100 ms, 2 % short at 20 ms, 17 % short at 5 ms. So a trace is followed to about 20 ms, and a finer trace is a restriction that needs `allow-limited`. Both shapers count Ethernet frames (14 bytes of header), like `netem`'s rate: one TCP flow through 8 mbit delivers 7,645 to 7,657 kbit/s, which is 1448/1514 of the rate to the digit.
- **The order of the stages is kept** (D-030): loss, then the rate with its queue, then the delay with the jitter. A shaper or a `netem` with a shaper as its child puts the delay in front of the rate, and with a saturated queue the shaper then absorbs the jitter: the standard deviation of the gaps between arrivals was 0.02 ms (gaps of exactly one packet time) against 5.7 ms with the jitter configured at 5 ms. The queue and the delay therefore sit on two different devices: the packet leaves the node, crosses the **fabric**, and comes out again.
- **The build.** `node A -- veth -- fabric -- veth -- node B`, one fabric namespace per run holding the middle of every such link, the two ports joined by a `mirred` redirect in both directions (a bridge was as slow or slower and needs a forwarding table). At the node the egress queue is a `netem` root with the loss (and duplication and corruption) and **a delay of 1 microsecond**, then the `htb` class with its leaf; in the fabric the egress queue towards the other node holds the delay, the jitter and the reordering. Frames cross unchanged, so the fixed neighbours and the MACs stay as set. A link that needs none of this keeps the direct build of releases 0.1 and 0.2, so earlier measurements stay valid; the scenario decides (a `queue` key, a step or a trace that sets a rate), and the result says which build each link got.
- **Why the delay of 1 microsecond.** A datagram stays charged to the socket of the sender until it leaves the queue, so a queue on the sending node pushes back: with the default `SO_SNDBUF` four seconds of traffic took 15.2 s, the queue never dropped, and the buffer cannot be raised in a node (`wmem_max` is absent or read-only there, `SO_SNDBUFFORCE` is refused to a user namespace). The kernel releases the packet in `netem` only if the `netem` has a delay, a jitter or a rate; a `netem` without any of them, and a veth hop, do not. With `delay 1us` (or `rate 100gbit`) in front, the sender keeps its pace (3.998 s of 4.0) and the queue drops as it should. The build depends on that detail of the kernel, so the fabric test has a canary: a first stage without a delay must show the push back.
- **Measured costs.** One TCP flow without a limit (offloads off): 7.2 and 8.8 Gbit/s direct, 5.3 and 6.4 through the fabric (27 % less); a packet is 0 to 12 microseconds later. The seeded loss is the same through the fabric (198 lost, the same hash, both images). The node-side `down` gives the sender `Network is unreachable` and leaves the peer up. `htb` wants `quantum` or `r2q` set for the rate. The doctor probes `cls_u32`, `act_mirred`, `htb` and the discipline.
- **Offloads are off on every veth** (`gso`, `tso`, `gro`): with them on, `netem` drops a super-packet of up to 44 segments at once and TCP ran 40 to 60 % over the model of Mathis (ratio 1.03 to 1.62 against 0.88 to 1.13 with them off).
- **`limit` is a number of packets waiting.** `limit 12` gave a median delay of 112 ms in a `netem` tree and 134 ms behind an `htb` with `pfifo limit 12`; the format fixes the meaning and a test pins it.

Alternatives: the delay in front of the shaper below a `netem` root (simple, one pair per link, but the jitter is destroyed at a saturated queue and the stage order of D-030 is lost), `tbf` (bucket refill at every step), `netem` with a rate (D-070), an `ifb` device (the capture would see each packet twice), a bridge in the fabric (slower), an inner veth pair in the fabric (does not release the packet from the socket).
