# D-071: Poisson, on/off and bursty traffic are three kinds, drawn from a seeded generator that is written out, and a schedule is walked and not looked up

Accepted (2026-11-02). WRK-02 asks for traffic that is not a constant stream: requests that arrive at random, a source that is silent for a while, packets that come in bursts. The decisions that are hard to undo are the names in the file, the algorithm that turns a seed into moments, and where the moments are computed.

- **Three kinds, not a `pattern` key.** `udp-poisson` (`rate` is the mean rate), `udp-onoff` (`rate` while on, `on`, `off`) and `udp-bursty` (`burst-packets`, `gap`, `pause`; no `rate`, because the mean rate follows and the result reports it), each with `id`, `from`, `to`, `packet-size`, `start`, `stop` and `udp-checksum` like `udp-cbr`. A key that belongs to another kind is refused at its own line with the kinds it belongs to. `distribution: fixed | exponential` (default `fixed`) says whether the phases and pauses are exactly what the file says, which a test can work out by hand, or drawn with that mean (a Markov source). The same word as the jitter, `distribution`, with values that fit this place. A burst is *packets, gap, pause*: `pause` counts from the last packet of a burst to the first of the next, so bursts cannot overlap whatever is drawn; the start-to-start interval that I first offered cannot say what an exponential value means when it is shorter than the burst.
- **The model.** `Workload` and `WorkloadKind` gained three variants each and are `non_exhaustive` from now on (the next kinds, `tcp-bulk` and `request-response`, will not break anybody again). That is a real break of the API of `netscenario-model` and `netscenario-core` (`series::Input` takes a `Schedule`, a `Generator` replaces `&UdpCbr` in the run), so the pull request is `feat!`. `Workload::generator()` gives a `Generator` (the keys that every UDP sender has, and a `Pattern`), so that the run, the validation and the backend do not repeat a `match` over the kinds. A feature `TrafficPatterns` in the coverage matrix, which a backend without the agent can leave out while it runs `udp-cbr`.
- **One schedule, walked.** The moment of packet `n` of a constant stream is a formula; of a Poisson source it depends on the gaps before it. `netscenario_agent::schedule::Schedule` is one of four patterns with `offsets(duration)`, an iterator in order. The sender walks it once and keeps nothing (a million packets a second for ten minutes would be 4.8 GB as a table). The run walks it again where it needs a moment: the series (one pass, a bin for each slot), and the packet that a step of the timeline waits for (one pass for all the steps of a workload, `packets_before_each`). Both ends get the same moments because the walk is a function of the schedule and the seed, and the packets are numbered in the order of the moments, so a barrier is still "before packet `n`".
- **The generator is ours.** SplitMix64 turns the seed into the state of xoshiro256++, a uniform number is `((x >> 11) + 1) × 2^-53`, an exponential one `-ln(u) × mean`, and `ln` is computed from the bits of the double with additions, multiplications and divisions only. The `rand` crates promise no stream across versions, and the `ln` of the standard library calls the maths library of the machine, which may differ in the last bit between systems; a result that is repeated on another machine must repeat the packets. The order of the draws is defined (the page `AGENT.md`), moments are `floor` of a sum kept as a double so that rounding does not pile up, and the first outputs are pinned in tests and in the page, from an implementation in Python that shares no code with the Rust (SplitMix64 from 0, xoshiro256++ from 1, 2, 3, 4 and from the seed 42, the derived seeds, the first moments of a Poisson, an on/off and a bursty source). The tests for the distributions use four standard errors (the mean and the median of 200,000 exponential numbers, the share of gaps under the mean, the variance of counts in windows, the pauses of 130,000 bursts) and each has a canary that a constant stream fails.
- **The seed.** Workload `n` (counted among the UDP senders) draws from `derive_seed(seed of the run, n)`: unrelated sequences for two workloads, and none shared between runs with different seeds. A scenario without a seed gets a drawn one as before; now also when the scenario has a source that draws and the backend cannot seed its queues: the agent is ours, so the traffic is repeatable on every backend, and the queues are then built without a seed and `loss_reproducible` says so, as it did. A source with fixed phases draws nothing.
- **What the result says.** `kind` is the kind, and `rate_bits_per_second` is the rate that the schedule is meant to give: the scenario's for `udp-cbr` and `udp-poisson`, the mean over the phases for `udp-onoff` (`rate × on / (on + off)`), the mean over a burst and its pause for `udp-bursty`. The command line says `on average` for these. The series, the recovery and the barriers work on the schedule and needed no new idea.
- **A Poisson source is not paced.** Its gaps can be shorter than the agent can keep (it was measured to 100,000 packets a second): such a packet leaves late and `max_lateness_ns` says by how much. The model refuses a rate above that and a gap in a burst below 10 us, so the mean is always within what was measured.
- **Tested.** Unit: the generator (reference outputs, the logarithm against the library, the distributions), the schedules (pinned moments, exact phases and bursts by hand, a prefix property: a shorter walk is the beginning of a longer one, the walk for several moments against counting, schedules that cannot send), the options of the agent, the sender (the packets of the schedule leave; a schedule without packets is refused), the model (each kind read, written, built, refused with the place; the schema), the core (the seed drawn for random traffic on a backend that cannot seed, the packet a step is bound to in a drawn schedule, the series of a drawn schedule, the features, the words that start the sender read back by the agent's parser). In real namespaces (`patterns.rs`): the sender sends exactly the packets of the schedule and none of them early, an on/off source whose cycle is a bin fills every bin with 200 packets, the same seed twice gives the same bursts and another does not, a step lands before the packet that the drawn schedule names and the lost packets repeat in two runs.

Alternatives: one `udp` kind with a `pattern` key (the keys of the patterns have nothing in common, and an error could not say which belong where); the new kinds inside `UdpCbr` (no break, but a type that lies about what it is, and the next kind repeats the problem); a closed form for the moment of packet `n` by splitting time into slots with a Poisson count in each (needs the prefix sum of the counts, so no random access either, and it is not a Poisson process at the edges of a slot); a table of moments (memory in proportion to the packets); `rand` and `rand_distr` (no promise of the stream across versions, and a dependency to audit for one function); the `ln` of the standard library (differs between systems); a random phase at the start of an on/off source (a start in an on phase can be worked out by hand, and the first packet of the run is the first packet of the pattern).
