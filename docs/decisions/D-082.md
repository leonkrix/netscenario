# D-082: The queue of a direction is the packets that wait for its rate, with a discipline and its parameters, and the run reads its counters

Accepted (2026-11-04). Release 0.3 adds the queue limit and the disciplines (LNK-09) and the counters of the queues (PRB-10). D-079 fixes how the kernel builds such a link; this entry fixes what the key means, what is refused, and what the result carries.

- **`queue` is a key of a direction** (`forward`, `reverse`, `both`, and the values of a shortcut). `discipline` is `fifo` (the default), `codel`, `fq-codel` or `pie`; `limit` is `100p` (packets) or `64kb` (data, `fifo` only); `target`, `interval`, `ecn` for the AQM disciplines, `flows` and `quantum` for `fq-codel`, `t-update` for `pie`. What is not set is the default of the kernel for the discipline, and the result records what was built.
- **The queue holds the packets that wait for the rate, not those in the delay behind it.** The delay of the link comes after the queue (D-030, D-079), so a deep queue adds its waiting time to the delay of the link, as it does in a router. The consequence is a rule of the format: a `queue` on a direction without a `rate` is refused, because nothing waits there and the key would do nothing. A packet is dropped when `limit` packets are waiting (`fifo`: tail drop), or by the rule of the discipline.
- **A parameter that belongs to another discipline is refused** with the discipline it belongs to (`interval` on a `fifo`, `t-update` on `codel`), a limit in bytes on a discipline that counts packets is refused, and so are values outside what the kernel takes (1 microsecond to 10 seconds, `flows` 1 to 65536, `quantum` up to 1 MiB, at most 1,000,000 packets or 1 GiB). The Kernel backend would otherwise ignore them, and a backend never approximates silently.
- **A step of the timeline cannot set `queue`.** The tree of a queue is built once and kept for the whole run (D-059); changing a discipline in the middle would empty the queue, which no network does. The error says so and points to the link, where the queue is written.
- **The counters are in the result, per direction with a queue** (`queues` in the result, `QueueInfo`): packets, bytes, drops, overlimits, requeues and the backlog at the end, the discipline and the kernel's name for it, and the counters special to the discipline (`ecn_mark`, `drop_overlimit`, `new_flow_count`, ...). The run also looks at them once a second while the workloads go (`samples`: the time from the start of the measured phase, packets, drops, backlog), so a test can ask whether the queue stayed short or when the first drop came. A run that may wait at a barrier for a step of the timeline takes no samples (the loop that answers the barriers is not held up), and a run shorter than a second has none; the counters at the end are always there. A backend that cannot say leaves the list empty.
- **Capabilities.** `QueueLimit` and `QueueDisciplines` are two features: a kernel without `htb`, `cls_u32` or `mirred` refuses both, a kernel without `codel`, `fq_codel` or `pie` refuses the disciplines that need it, each with the module to load, and the doctor lists both as checks (`queues`, `disciplines`).
- **The accounting is unchanged** (D-040, D-080): a queue that drops is the `lost_in_queue` of its direction.

Alternatives: let `queue` stand without `rate` and give it the meaning of the `netem` limit (it would count the packets in the delay, which was the cause of lost packets nobody asked for, see `docs/COVERAGE.md`); allow `queue` in a step (a change of the limit alone is possible with `tc`, a change of the discipline is not, so the key would be half a feature); one flat `queue: 100p` for the limit and `aqm:` for the rest (two keys for one thing).
