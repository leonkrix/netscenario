# D-048: What the `netem` parameters mean, as measured, and which of them the format does not offer

Accepted (2026-10-31, after the spike S12, `spikes/s12_models.py`, 100,000 packets in each of three seeds for the loss models, 20,000 to 100,000 for the rest). The measured value is the mean of the seeds; it is judged against three times the difference between the seeds.

- **Confirmed against their mathematics:** `loss random` (1, 10 and 30 percent: loss and mean burst length), `loss gemodel` (three parameter sets: loss, and the mean burst length where it is the stay in the bad state), `loss state` (two sets: the stationary loss and the mean burst length of the four-state chain), `duplicate` (5 and 20 percent), `reorder` (the share of packets sent on at once, without and with `gap`). These are the cells of the fidelity cases of the impairments of release 0.2.
- **`gemodel p r 1-h 1-k` means:** `p` the probability to go from the good to the bad state, `r` back, the third argument the **loss probability in the bad state** and the fourth the loss probability in the good state (the letters `h` and `k` would suggest the complements; the measurement agrees with the first reading in all three cases: 4.6 against 4.8 percent predicted, and 95 percent for the other reading). The format names them `loss-bad` and `loss-good`, as the sketch of SCENARIO-FORMAT has them. In `loss state p13 p31 p32 p23 p14` the states are 1 receiving in a gap, 2 receiving inside a burst, 3 losing inside a burst, 4 losing a single packet in a gap, and the five numbers are the transitions in that order; the chain written down this way predicts the measurement.
- **`reorder P` sends a share P of the packets on at once and holds the rest for the delay;** the held ones are the ones that arrive after a packet with a higher number. With a delay much larger than the gap between packets the count of RFC 4737 is therefore about 1 - P (74.9 percent for `reorder 25%`, 87.5 percent with `gap 5`), not P. With `gap g` the share sent on at once is 1 / (g - 1 + 1 / P) (12.5 percent for 25 percent and `gap 5`, measured 12.49). RESULTS.md and COVERAGE.md say what `reordered` counts. `tc` itself refuses `reorder` without a `delay` ("reordering not possible without specifying some delay"), and the scenario reader refuses it earlier, with a message that names the key.
- **The correlation argument is not offered.** The second argument of `loss random`, `duplicate` and `reorder` does not keep the probability: `loss random 10% 50%` lost 0.1 percent and `... 90%` lost none, `reorder 25% 50%` sent 7.0 percent on at once and `... 90%` none, and `duplicate 20% 50%` and `... 90%` gave exactly the result of no correlation (the argument has no effect). A backend that approximates silently is what the rules forbid, and there is no correct reading of "10 percent loss with correlation 50 percent" that this produces. Bursts are asked for with `gemodel` and `state`, which are verified. The same may hold for the correlation of the delay (`delay 10ms 2ms 25%`); it is not offered either until it is measured.
- **A change keeps every setting that it does not mention.** Measured by changing a fresh `netem` to `delay 1ms` and reading it back: `corrupt`, `reorder`, `rate` and `slot` stayed as they were; `loss` (also `gemodel` and `state`), `duplicate`, the jitter and its distribution were reset. Writing the value zero resets each of the four (`corrupt 0%`, `reorder 0%`, `rate 0bit`, `slot 0`). A timeline that changes a link must therefore write the **whole state** of the direction in every change, with an explicit zero for these four, or a corruption or a rate from an earlier step would stay in force without a word in the scenario (this showed in S13b: a run asked only for `loss 10%` corrupted 8.5 percent of its packets, left over from the run before). The argument builder of the backend does it, and a test changes from every state to every other and compares the result with a fresh qdisc (with a canary that omits the zeros and must fail). Deleting and adding the qdisc would also reset, but it empties the queue and starts the random generator again, which D-038 forbids.
- **`duplicate P` clones a packet with probability P, and the clone passes every other impairment on its own.** With nothing else set, 20 percent gave 20.0 percent over 100,000 packets (19.98 on this machine). While a corruption of 10 percent was left over by mistake in the first run of the workflow, the same 20 percent gave 16.3 percent, which is 0.2 x 0.9 x 0.9: both copies must survive the corruption, each on its own. With a loss of 10 percent a duplicated packet is lost twice as often as it is lost once, and a result must not be read as "20 percent of the packets were doubled" when other impairments act. (That run also showed the rule above by accident: its correlation section lost 18.7 percent instead of 10 because of the leftover corruption. The spike now starts every measurement with a fresh qdisc.)
- **The figures do not depend on the kernel.** The lost packets of every seeded series are the same to the digit on the kernels 6.8, 6.12 and 7.0 (for instance `gemodel` 4.607 percent).
- **Corruption** is D-040.

Alternatives: offering the correlation with a warning (the number the user wrote would not be the number that happens); correcting the probability in our code (a model of our own that nobody can check against the manual).
