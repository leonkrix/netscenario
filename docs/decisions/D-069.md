# D-069: The bound on the lateness of a step follows what the machine does to a sleeping thread

Accepted (2026-11-02). A cleaning of a bound that was a choice and not a derivation (D-060 said so). The tests of the timeline judge that no step is later than 50 ms. Under 24 busy loops pinned to 4 cores (six runnable threads on each) a step was 50.45 ms late in 2 of 6 runs, and a run next to a build had one at 98 ms: the machine, not the code.

- **The rule.** A step is done by a thread of the run that wakes up for it, so it cannot be expected to be later than the machine makes any thread late, plus the work of the step (0.2 to 0.4 ms through the whole path, D-063). The test measures the first before the run: the worst overshoot of 150 sleeps of 1 ms (about 0.15 s). The bound is 50 ms (the old one, and the floor), or four times that overshoot plus 5 ms if that is more. The lower bound stays exact: a step by the clock is never done before its moment, and the test fails if one began early.
- **Measured.** With the fixed bound, 2 of 6 runs of the test of the order of a `both` failed under the load above. With the bound that follows the machine, all 13 tests of the timeline passed in 4 runs out of 4 under the same load, and on a quiet machine the bound is the 50 ms it was.
- **What it can still miss.** A machine that is quiet while it is measured and stalls for longer than four times the worst of 150 sleeps during the run. That is a stall of the whole machine, and the tests then fail with the lateness in the message instead of being widened for ever.
- **The canary** judges made-up records (80 ms late, early, backwards, ended before it began) against the 50 ms floor and must name each.

Alternatives: a larger fixed bound (every machine would be judged by the worst one, and a broken scheduler would pass); no bound on the upper side (the lateness is in the result for a reader, but a test that cannot fail proves nothing); the median instead of the worst overshoot (the stalls are the point).

**Addendum (2026-11-02).** The lower bound was not exact after all: the run took the moment of the beginning twice, as an `Instant` and as `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` nanoseconds, in two reads one after the other, and waited for the steps of the clock on the first while recording them on the second. A stall of 3 µs between the reads made a step "begin 3135 ns before it may" (once in 567 tests, Ubuntu 22.04 in the CI). The steps are now waited for on the clock of the records (`Begin::left_until`), so the bound is exact by construction, and a unit test counts a moment from a beginning that is far from any `Instant`.
