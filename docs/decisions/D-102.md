# D-102: Two backends are compared by equivalence inside a margin that is derived from what each backend measures about itself, not by a test of "no difference"

Accepted (2026-11-12), for BKD-07 and the second acceptance criterion of 0.4. S23 ran the same UDP link (10,000 packets per second, 4 s, three runs) on the kernel (`netem` on the client's interface) and on the engine with two timers (the three links: a delay of 20 ms; a delay of 20 ms with a uniform jitter of 5 ms; a loss of 10 %).

- **What S23 showed.** The distributions are the same in shape: delay 20 ms, jitter 5 ms: mean 19.98 to 20.01 ms (kernel) against 19.98 to 20.04 (engine), standard deviation 2.88 to 2.90 against 2.89 to 2.92 (the model gives 2.887), minimum 15.00 against 15.00 to 15.01, 99th percentile 24.90 to 24.91 against 24.91 to 24.97; loss 9.77 to 10.09 % against 10.14 to 10.27 % of 40,000 packets (one standard deviation of a binomial count is 0.15 %). But the tails and the base differ: with a fixed delay the kernel has a standard deviation of 1 to 2 microseconds and a maximum 0.03 to 0.05 ms above the delay, the engine with spin-sleep 3 microseconds and a maximum of 0.09 to 0.2 ms, the engine with `epoll` 0.11 to 0.25 ms and a maximum of 3.6 to 4.1 ms above; and the hop of the engine adds a base delay of 6 microseconds (spin-sleep) to 12 (`epoll`). Any test of "the two distributions are equal" rejects such a pair at a sample of a few thousand packets, and would pass an engine that is wrong by less than the noise of its own timer.
- **The method.** For each quantile (0.5, 0.9, 0.99) of the delay, the difference of the engine's and the kernel's quantile has a percentile-bootstrap interval (D-074) over the runs of a set, and the comparison passes when the interval lies inside **plus or minus the margin**. Loss, duplication and corruption are counts: the rates are compared by their intervals (the exact binomial), with a margin of zero plus the interval's own width. Throughput is compared on the goodput of a bin. A rank test (Mann-Whitney) is printed as a diagnostic and is not judged.
- **The margin is derived, not picked.** For a quantile q it is `L(q) + B + K`: L(q) is the lateness quantile that the engine reports in the very same run (D-098, so a loose timer widens its own margin and says so); B is the base delay of the hop, measured by a comparison with no impairment in the same set; K is the allowance for the kernel path, the spread of kernel against kernel measured in the set (30 pairs of runs). The margin is written next to each comparison in `docs/DIFFERENTIAL.md` with the numbers it came from.
- **The level.** Each comparison gets a level so that one run of the CI has a false alarm below 1e-6 over all of them (the Bonferroni share); the sizes of the sets follow from the level and the margins.
- **Canaries.** An engine built with the feature `fault-injection` (only for tests: a delay too long by 5 %, a loss rate 1.1 times too high, a jitter 20 % too wide, a rate 5 % too high) must fail exactly the comparison that it should; the kernel against the kernel passes 30 times in 30.
- **What "statistically equal for UDP" then means** in `docs/ACCEPTANCE.md`: for the scenarios listed there, no quantile or rate differs by more than its margin, at the stated level, and the margins are the ones that the machines produced. It does not mean that the engine is as tight as the kernel; the tables say how much looser it is.

Alternatives: a two-sample test of equality (rejects a good pair and passes a bad one); fixed tolerances picked by eye (the rule of this project is that bounds are derived); comparing packet by packet (the generators differ by design, D-099).
